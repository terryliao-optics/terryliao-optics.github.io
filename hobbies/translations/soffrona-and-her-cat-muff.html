Soffrona and her Cat Muff

中文
English

Soffrona and her Cat Muff

小时候，索夫罗娜与一位非常爱她的夫人住在一起。她并非这位夫人的亲生骨肉，但对方视她如同己出，索夫罗娜也常称呼她为妈妈。夫人有一位六岁的亲生女儿名叫索菲亚，她比索夫罗娜大一岁，二人形影不离，她们在一起上课，游戏，彼此之间的陪伴让她们非常快乐。如果你看到索夫罗娜，那索菲亚很可能就在附近，反之亦然。

LITTLE Soffrona lived with a lady who loved her very much. She was not the lady’s own child, but she was as dear to that lady as if she had been so, and the child always called her mamma. The lady had a little girl of her own called Sophia. Sophia was one year older than Soffrona; and Sophia and Soffrona learned lessons together, and played together, and were very happy in each other’s company. When you saw Soffrona, you might be sure Sophia was not very far off; and when you saw Sophia, it was very certain that Soffrona was at no great distance.

对于小孩子来说，与另一个同龄人度过充满爱与平和的童年时光是多么愉快啊！正如《圣经》中大卫所言：看哪，弟兄们一起住在一起是多么美好和愉快啊！

How delightful it is for little children to live in love and peace one with another! Hear what David says on this subject——Behold, how good and pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!

索夫罗娜与索菲亚住在一间十分可爱的房子里，那里树林环绕，无论从哪扇窗户看出去，树木都枝繁叶茂，宛如漂亮的凉亭，为身下蜿蜒其中的小路带去清凉的阴影；树林边零星散布着几处山涧，奔流注入山谷之中：在那边有一些小山坡，其中有些山顶整个冬天都被积雪覆盖，尽管到了夏天，或是一年中的某些时候，它们会变回绿色或者蓝色，如同穿了一件好看的外衣。

Soffrona and Sophia lived in a very lovely house, surrounded with woods. Wherever you looked from the windows of that house, you might see trees growing thickly together, forming beautiful arbours, and pleasant shades, with little paths winding about among those trees; and here and there, near the trees, were fountains of water springing from the hills, and running down into the valleys: for there were hills there, and the tops of some of them were covered all through the winter with snow, though in summer they appeared green or blue, according to the time of the year, and wore a very pleasant aspect.

两个小姑娘可以在树林间玩乐，她们已然学会了像小鹿一样一路跑着跳过那些小山坡。看到她们如此快乐会令人心旷神怡，在树林中，她们找到了许多宝藏，比如蜗牛壳，色彩斑斓的石头，还有野生的草莓，越橘，核桃，榛子，漂亮的苔藓和各式各样的花朵，对于没去过树林里的孩子来说这些宝藏是闻所未闻的；在那里，她们可以聆听鸟儿们合奏的演唱会，参演者包括了杜鹃，红雀，黑鹂与画眉；她们还看到美丽的蝴蝶有着金紫色的羽翅，龙蝇的翅膀看起来像丝绸一样光滑。

Soffrona and Sophia were allowed to play in these woods, and they had learned to run and skip upon the hills like young fawns. It was very pleasing to see them, and they found many treasures in those wild places which children who have never been in woods have no idea of. They found snail-shells, and painting-stones, and wild strawberries, and bilberries, and walnuts, and hazel nuts, and beautiful moss, and many kinds of flowers; and there they heard birds sing—cuckoos, and linnets, and blackbirds, and thrushes; and saw beautiful butterflies with gold and purple plumes, and dragon-flies, whose wings look like fine silk net.

五月的一个早晨，索夫罗娜与索菲亚获准在上完课后去树林里面玩，为了把在树林发现的宝藏带回家去，她们带了一个篮子。他们在树林中走了很长一段路，至少有半英里，直到在一条小溪边发现一棵被吹倒的老树才停下来。索夫罗娜与索菲亚坐下来，各自从篮子里拿出一本小人书读了起来。正当她们读着书时，一阵男孩子们的吵闹声夹杂着大喊大笑传了过来。索夫罗娜与索菲亚从老树上跳了下来并躲在了灌木丛的后面。

One morning in the month of May, Soffrona and Sophia had leave given to them to play in the woods, after they had finished their lessons, and they took a basket with them, to bring home any treasures which they might find. And they went a long way through the woods,—I dare say as much as half a mile,—till they came to a place where an old tree had been blown down by the side of a brook; and there they sat down, and each of them took a little penny book to read out of their basket: and while they were reading, they heard a noise of boys shouting and laughing, and they jumped up and hid themselves behind some bushes.

男孩子们来到小溪边上，索夫罗娜与索菲亚听到他们中的一个说，“我们把它放到水最深的地方，这样它就爬不出来了。” 随后她们看见男孩们伏在水下并把什么东西放在了那里，与此同时，她们听到了一声小猫的哀嚎；两个小姑娘再也不能坐视不管了，她们从灌木丛中大声喊道，“你们这些邪恶残忍的家伙！你们在干什么？”

So the boys came nearer, and went down close to the water’s side; and the little girls heard them say one to another, “Let us put it in the deepest place, where it cannot scramble out.” And they saw the boys stoop over the water and put something into it, and at the same time they heard a very young kitten cry; and the two little girls could not stop themselves from screaming out, quite loud, from the midst of the bushes, saying, “Wicked, cruel boys! what are you doing?”

男孩们听到小姑娘们的控诉后，如同圣经所言：恶人虽无人追赶也会逃跑，他们都催动脚步，迅速地逃离了现场，只留下可怜的小猫仍淹在水中。

Now the boys heard the cries of the little girls; and, as the Bible says, The wicked flee when no man pursueth; (Prov. xxviii. 1.) so they all took to their heels, and ran away as fast as they could, leaving the poor little kitten in the water.

索夫罗娜与索菲亚片刻也没有耽搁，直奔小溪而去，发现小猫已然气若游丝。然而，尽管会弄湿自己地膝盖，她们还是将小猫从水里捞了上来，回到了老树边。索夫罗娜坐下来，把小猫放到了自己的大腿上，索菲亚则擦干了小猫身上的水，在擦拭的过程中，她发现小猫的身体又有了温度，不久，小猫睁开了眼睛开始喵喵叫了起来。“可爱的猫咪”，索夫罗娜感叹道“看到你没事真是太好了！我想把你留下，就叫你Muff吧。索菲亚，你可以把它让给我吧，可以的吧？”

Soffrona and Sophia did not lose one moment after the boys were gone, but ran to the brook, and found the little kitten almost dead. However, they got it out, though they wet themselves up to the knees in so doing, and they returned to the tree, and Soffrona sat down, and laid it upon her lap, while Sophia wiped it dry; and as she rubbed it, she found warmth returning to its little body, and presently it opened its eyes and began to mew. “O my dear little Puss!” said Soffrona, “how very glad I am that you are not dead! You shall be my Puss, and I will call you Muff. Will you let her be mine, Sophia? Will you give me your share of her?”

面对这个请求，索菲亚什么都没说，在她看来这只小猫跟着自己还是跟着索夫罗娜都是一回事，她很乐意成全索夫罗娜。更何况，索菲亚自认自己比索夫罗娜大一岁，就应该让着妹妹，多考虑考虑对方。索菲亚对世界的认知比索夫罗娜早十二个月，即使带着上帝的祝福，一个人能在十二个月里学会什么呢？

Sophia did not say a word against this request, for it was the same to her whether the little kitten was called hers or Soffrona’s, and she liked to oblige Soffrona: besides, Sophia was a year older than Soffrona, and it might be expected that she would be more moderate in her desires, and think less of herself. Sophia had lived twelve months longer than Soffrona in the world; and how much may a person learn, with the blessing of God, in twelve months!

就这样，小猫Muff归属于索夫罗娜了；当小姑娘们尽其所能为它擦干身体后，就将它放在铺着些许柔软苔藓的篮子里，一路跑着回家了。

So it was agreed that the kitten should belong to Soffrona, and be called Muff; and when the little girls had dried it as well as they could,they put it into the basket upon some soft moss, and ran home with it.

夫人迅速地给她们换了衣服，对于小姑娘们为了救下小猫而踏入小溪弄湿了自己这件事，她并未感到愠怒。她们将小猫从篮子里拿出来，用牛奶喂它。

The lady was not angry with them for having wetted themselves in the brook to save a poor little animal’s life, but she hastened to change their clothes; and then they took the kitten out of the basket, and procured some milk to feed it with.

当小猫的身上彻底干燥下来，它身上美丽的花纹才完全显露出来。它的腿、脸和胸部都很白，背上有黄色和黑色的条纹，就像一块被精心打磨过的玳瑁壳。然而这只小猫看起来出生了也就10天左右，还没办法喝牛奶；这让索夫罗娜与索菲亚十分难过，如此一来，尽管Muff逃离了溺毙的命运，却终会被饥饿折磨致死。小姑娘们试着用勺子将牛奶送进小猫的喉咙，但牛奶却顺着嘴角流了下来。由于缺乏营养，这个小生命的身体两侧渐渐消瘦了下来。

When the fur of the little cat was quite dry, it was seen that she was very beautifully marked. Her legs, and face, and breast, were quite white, and her back was streaked with yellow and black; so that she appeared like a fine polished tortoise-shell. But she was only nine or ten days old, and was not able to lap milk; and this was a great grief to Soffrona and Sophia, for they feared that although she had been saved from the water, she would surely die of hunger. The little girls tried to force milk down her throat with a spoon; but the milk ran down the outside of her mouth, instead of the inside of her throat, and the little creature’s sides became quite hollow for want of nourishment.

索夫罗娜为了Muff彻夜未眠，她将小猫放在自己的大腿上，无论时读书还是吃晚餐都不曾中断。她全部的小小身心已经被Muff所占据，当夫人让她给一位住在附近的山中小屋里却又穷困清苦的老妇人织一件法兰绒衬裙时，她虽然确确实实将针拿在了手里，却很难说有多少心思在衬裙上，实际上她的目光时常望向她躺在大腿上的小猫身上，若不是索菲亚那天晚上加倍的勤奋，针织衬裙的工作根本没法完成。

Soffrona was thinking of nothing but Muff all the evening, and she kept her on her lap while she was reading and while she was eating her supper. She was, indeed, so much occupied by her little kitten, that, when the lady asked her to help to make a flannel petticoat for a poor old woman who lived in a cottage among the hills, not very far off, she took the needle in her hand, it is true, but I do not think that she took twenty stitches; for she was looking down every minute upon the kitten on her lap: and the petticoat would not have been done that night, if Sophia had not been doubly diligent.

如今只能多多祈祷那件衬裙在当天晚上就完成了，那位住在森林里的老妇人善良和蔼，却又十分可怜，她完全指望这件衬裙来帮助他抵御三月的寒风，那使她的四肢疼痛难忍，这件衬裙对于她来说必不可少。更重要的是，夫人也答应两个小姑娘，明早完成功课后，就将茶叶，糖果还有这条衬裙带到小屋去。但是，如前所述，索夫罗娜心系小猫，完全没法考虑其他事情，遑论其他生命。她的心中对老妇人没什么怜悯之情，但却对Muff十分关切。我们是应该善待动物，但我们首先应该将造物主排在第一位，将同类排在第二，其次才是那些我们力所能及能够救助的可怜动物。

Now it was much to be wished that the petticoat should be done that night; for it was intended for a good old woman who lived in the woods, a very poor woman indeed, and the March winds had given her great pain in her limbs, and she was in much need of a warm petticoat; and, more than that, the lady had promised the little girls the pleasure of taking the petticoat, with some tea and sugar, the next morning, after they had repeated their lessons, to the cottage. But, as I before said, Soffrona’s heart was with her kitten, and she could think of nothing else, and of no other creature. She had no pity left for the old woman, so much was she thinking of little Muff. We ought to be kind to animals; but our first affections should be given to our Maker, our second to our fellow-creatures, and our third to any poor animals which may be in our power.

索夫罗娜睡前最后一件事是尝试给Muff喂奶，第二天一早她又尝试这么做，就目前来说，她做的没错，这个可怜的小生命完全依赖于她。但当她做了所有她能做的事后，她理当肩负起其他的责任。但在那天早晨，索夫罗娜无法强迫自己去做其他事情，她的功课做得很糟糕，如果不是夫人的宽容溺爱，她就会失去和索菲亚一起去看老妇人穿上衬裙的快乐了。

The last thing Soffrona did in the evening, was to try to put some milk down Muff’s throat, and this was the first thing she did in the morning: and so far she did right, for the poor little thing depended on her. But when she had done all she could for Muff, she should have given her mind to her other duties; but she could not command herself to attend to any thing else all that morning, and learned her lessons so ill, that, if the lady had not been very indulgent, she would have deprived her of the pleasure of walking with Sophia to see the old woman, and to carry the petticoat.

夫人家有位衣着得体的年轻女仆Jane，她经常在索夫罗娜与索菲亚要离家很远的时候伴行左右，那天早上，两个小姑娘的功课时间结束时，jane已经做好准备了。

There was a neat little maid-servant, called Jane, who used to walk out with Sophia and Soffrona when they had a long way to go; and Jane was ready waiting for the little girls by the time the lessons were done.

索菲亚要求提上了装有衬裙，茶叶和糖的篮子，而索夫罗娜则另外提了一个篮子以带上Muff，并在篮子底部铺了一点法兰绒同时盖上了盖子。当索菲亚提上自己的篮子，索夫罗娜也带上自己的篮子一同下了楼。

Sophia had asked leave to carry the basket with the petticoat and the tea and sugar; and Soffrona took another basket, and put a bit of flannel at the bottom of it, and laid Muff in it, and tied the cover over it; and when Sophia took up her basket to carry, Soffrona also put her arm under the handle of Muff’s basket, and went down stairs with it.

当她们出门后，Jane问道：“姑娘们，你们要给Martha奶奶带整整两篮子的好东西吗？我很高兴，她是位虔诚的老人。”

When they were got out of the house, Jane said, “What, have you two baskets, young ladies, full of good things, to carry to old Martha? Well, I am very glad; for she is a good and pious old woman.”

索夫罗娜因尴尬而脸红，沉默不答；索菲亚则笑了笑并说道：“她没在篮子里装给老奶奶的东西，她只带了Muff，里面还有铺好的法兰绒。”

Soffrona coloured, but did not answer; and Sophia smiled, and said, “She has not got any thing for the old woman in her basket: she has only got Muff, wrapped in flannel, in it.”

“哦，小姐！”简说：“你怎么能想去做这样的事呢？”你把小猫抱着是多麻烦啊！我们有两英里要路，大部分都要上山。请让我把小猫带回屋里。”

“O, Miss!” said Jane, “how can you think of doing such a thing? What a trouble it will be to you to carry the kitten all the way! and we have two miles to walk, and most of it up hill. Please to let me carry the kitten back to the house.”

“No, no, Jane,” said Soffrona, “no, you shall not.”

“Shall not, Miss!” said Jane: “is that a pretty word?”

“不，不，简，”索夫罗娜说，“不，你不可以正阳做。”

Soffrona looked very cross, and Jane was turning back to complain to the lady: but Sophia entreated her not to do it; and Soffrona submitted to ask her pardon for being rude, and promised to behave better, if she would permit her to carry the kitten where she was going. So that matter was settled, and Jane and the little girls proceeded.

“不可以，小姐！”简说：“这个词很得体吗？”

索夫罗娜看上去很生气，简转身向那位女士抱怨，但索菲娅恳求她不要这样做。索夫罗娜也请求她原谅她的粗鲁，并答应如果允许她把小猫抱到她要去的地方，她会表现得好些。事情这样才解决了，简和小女孩们继续向着目的地出发了。

I could tell you much about the pretty places through which they passed in going to poor Martha’s cottage, which were quite new to the little girls. They first went through some dark woods, where the trees met over their heads like the arches in a church; and then they came to a dingle, where water was running at the bottom, and they crossed the water by a wooden bridge; then they had to climb up such a steep, such a very steep hill, covered with bushes; then they came to a high field surrounded with trees, and in a corner of that field was old Martha’s thatched cottage. It was a poor place: the walls were black-and-white, and there were two windows, one of which was in the thatch, and one below, and a door, half of which was open; for it was such a door as you see in cottages, the lower part of which can be shut while the other is open. There was a little smoke coming out of the chimney,for Martha was cooking her potatoes for her dinner.

我可以向你们描述这一路上她们所路过的许多美丽风景，终点处Martha奶奶的小屋对姑娘们来说是个新奇的去处。她们首先穿过了一片幽暗的森林，树木的枝桠在他们的头顶上宛如教堂的穹顶，当她们从木桥上跨过峡谷时，溪水从谷底潺潺流过。在爬过一座被灌木丛覆盖着的十分陡峭的小山，又穿过一片高原上的树林后，在高原的一个角落里，Martha奶奶的茅草屋就坐落在那里。这是一个有些寒酸的住所，墙壁黑白，一扇窗户陷在茅草里面，另一扇则在屋子接地处，一扇半掩着的门，下面的部分像是门槛，可以在其他部分开着的时候紧闭，标准的村舍配置。炊烟袅袅，Martha奶奶正在用土豆为自己准备晚餐。

“Do you think Martha has any milk in her house?” said Soffrona; “for poor Muff must be very hungry by this time.”

“你说Martha奶奶家有牛奶吗？”索夫罗娜问道，“可怜的Muff，它估计饿坏了。”

“I fear not,” replied Jane: “but come, young ladies, we have been a long time getting up this hill, and we must be at home by three o’clock.”

“恐怕没有”Jane回复到：“但是姑娘们，得走快点，我们爬这座山太慢了，我们必须三点前回家。”

So they went on, and came close to the door, and stood there a little while, looking in. They saw within the cottage a very small kitchen; but it was neat, and there was nothing out of its place. There was a wide chimney in the kitchen; and in the chimney a fire of sticks, over which hung a little kettle. Old Martha was sitting on a stool within the chimney. She was dressed in a blue petticoat and jacket, and had a high crowned, old-fashioned felt hat on her head, and a coarse clean check handkerchief on her neck. Before her was a spinning-wheel, which she was turning very diligently, for she could not see to do any work besides spinning; and by the fire, on the hob, sat a fine tortoise-shell cat, which was the old woman’s only companion. “O!” cried little Soffrona, “there is a cat! I see a cat!”

于是一行人继续前行，渐渐靠近Martha奶奶家的门口，她们在那里停住了一下，向屋内望去。屋舍内有一间狭小却整洁的厨房，里面没什么东西。宽口的烟囱下口延申到厨房里，柴火在其中烧的正旺，其上有一个小水壶。Martha奶奶正坐在烟囱边的凳子上，她穿着一件蓝色的衬裙和夹克，头上戴着一顶老式的高顶的毡帽，脖子上围着一块粗糙却干净的格子手帕。老奶奶身前是一个纺车，正被她不辞辛苦地转动着，这也是她唯一能做的事情。在柴火边的锄头上，一只三花猫正坐在那里，它是Martha奶奶唯一的陪伴者。“哦！”小索夫罗娜惊呼，“一只猫，我看到一只猫！”

“Dear, Miss,” said Jane, “you can think of nothing but cats.”

“亲爱的小姐，”Jane说到，“你的心思全放在猫猫身上了。”

“Well, Jane,” answered Sophia, “and if she is fond of cats, is there any harm in it?”

“Jane，别大惊小怪的，”索菲亚辩解道，“她喜欢猫又没什么害处。”

Jane could make no answer, for by this time old Martha had seen them, and came halting on her crutch to meet them, and to offer them all the seats in her house; and these were only a three-legged stool and two old chairs.

Jane无言以对，就在此时Martha奶奶看见她们了，她顿住了拐杖来迎接客人，一面将家里的所有的座位都让给姑娘们。家里只有一张三条腿的凳子和两把旧椅子。

Sophia then presented the old woman with what she had brought from her mamma, and Jane gave her a bottle of medicine from her pocket: and the old woman spoke of the goodness of Almighty God, who had put it into the lady’s heart to provide her with what she needed most in this world.

索菲亚把妈妈让带给Martha奶奶的东西拿出来，Jane从自己的口袋里掏出一瓶药；老奶奶感谢来自全能上帝的仁慈，将她在这个世界上最需要的东西带给了她。

Now, while Sophia and Jane and Martha were looking over the things which the lady had sent, the old cat had left the hob, and had come to Soffrona, and was staring wildly, and mewing in a strange way round the basket; and at the same time the kitten within began to mew. “Puss! Puss! pretty Puss!” said Soffrona, for she was half afraid of this large cat, and yet at the same time very well inclined to form a friendship with her.

就在这时，正当索菲娅、Jane和Martha奶奶看夫人寄来的东西时，那只老猫离开了锄头，来到索夫罗娜身边，着了魔似地盯着篮子，绕着篮子喵喵叫。与此同时，里面的小猫开始喵喵叫。“猫咪！”猫咪！漂亮的猫咪！”索夫罗娜喊到，她有点害怕这只大猫，但同时也很想和她建立友谊。

At length, those that were with her in the cottage saw what was passing; and Martha said, “Don’t be afraid, Miss; Tibby won’t hurt you. Poor thing! she is in great trouble, and has been so ever since yesterday.”

终于，屋子里的其他人看到了这边的情况；Martha奶奶说：“别害怕，小姐；Tibby不会伤害你的。可怜的小家伙！她遇到了大麻烦，从昨天起就一直这样了。”

“What trouble?” said Soffrona.

“什么麻烦？”索夫罗娜说到。

“Some rude boys came in yesterday, and stole her kitten,” replied Martha. “I was in the wood, picking a few sticks, and left the door open; and the boys came in, and ran away with the kitten; and the poor cat has been moaning and grieving like a human being,—poor dumb thing,—ever since. The cruel lads! I saw them go down the hill!”

“有些粗鲁没素质的男孩昨天闯进来偷走了她的孩子，”Martha奶奶说到，“我当时在森林里捡柴火，没有关好门；那些男孩进来，抢了小猫就跑，它因此一直在悲伤地嚎叫，就像失去了孩子的母亲一样——可怜的孩子——，那些男孩子太无耻了，我看到他们跑下山去了。”

“O!” said Soffrona, “and I do believe”——

“And I am sure,” said Sophia.

“And I am so glad!” said Soffrona.

“And how happy she will be!” said Sophia.

“哦！”索夫罗娜喊了出来，“我觉得”——

And Soffrona immediately set down her basket and opened it, and put the little kitten on the floor, for the kitten was indeed poor Tibby’s kitten.

“我敢肯定！”索菲亚也说到。

“我太高兴了！”索夫罗娜说到。

“她该会是多么开心啊！”索菲亚补充到。

索夫罗娜立刻放下并打开了她的篮子，并将Muff放到了地上，它正是Tibby那可怜的孩子。

It was a pretty sight, an agreeable and pleasant sight, to behold the joy of the old cat when she saw her kitten. The poor creature seemed as if she would have talked. Martha took up the kitten, and laid it on a little bit of a mat in the corner of the chimney, where it used to be; and the old cat ran to it, and lay down by it, and gave it milk, and licked it, and talked to it in her way, (that is, in the way that cats use to their kittens,) and purred so loud, that you might have heard her to the very end of the cottage. It was a pleasant sight, as I said before, for it is a pleasure to see any thing happy; and Soffrona jumped and capered about the house, and knew not how sufficiently to express her joy: and as for little Sophia, her eyes were filled with tears; and poor old Martha was not the least happy of the party.

眼前是一幅和蔼温馨又快乐兴奋的景象，猫妈妈对能再次看到自己的孩子感到无比开心，看起来它的激动已经溢于言表。Martha奶奶抱起小猫，将它放到了烟囱角落的小垫子上，那也是它被抢走前所待的位置；Tibby飞奔过去，躺在那里给Muff喂奶，梳理它孩子的毛发，并用猫的语言和Muff交流，大声的说着什么，即使在小屋的另一边也清晰可闻。如前所述，这是十分动人温馨的场景，幸福的故事会带给听者幸福；索夫罗娜又蹦又跳，甚至不知道如何表达自己的快乐，索菲亚则喜极而泣，Martha奶奶反而成了聚会上最平静的一位。

And now, when it was time to go, Soffrona took up her empty basket, and giving the kitten a kiss, “Little Puss,” she said, “I will rejoice in your happiness, though it will be a loss to me, for I must part with my little darling. But I will not be selfish: mamma says that I can never make myself happy by making other things miserable. Good-bye, little Puss: if God will help me, I will try never to be selfish.” And she walked out of the cottage, wiping away her tears.

时间很快到了分别的时分，索夫罗娜挽起她空空的篮子，亲了亲小猫，“小猫咪，”索夫罗娜说到，“我为你的幸福而感到快乐，对我来说，尽管和你分别让我难过，但我知道我不能太自私。妈妈说过：不能将自己的快乐建立在他人的痛苦之上。再见了，小猫：若上帝能帮到我，我便会永不自私。”索夫罗娜一边擦去泪水，一边走出了Martha奶奶的小屋。

“But you will let her have Muff, won’t you, Martha,” said Sophia, “when her mother has brought her up, and can part with her?”

“To be sure I will, dear Miss,” replied Martha; “for I was delighted to hear her say that she knew she never could make herself happy by making others miserable.”

“但你可以在Muff成长到可以独自生活的时候再来陪着索夫罗娜，对吧，Martha奶奶？”索菲亚问道。

When Muff was a quarter old, she was brought to Soffrona, and became her cat, and lived in her service till her yellow and black hairs were mingled with grey.’

“我确信我一定会的，亲爱的，”Martha奶奶回答道：“听到她明白她不会用别人的苦难来换取自己的快乐，这让我感到欣慰。”

Muff在长到三个月大的时候成为了索夫罗娜的小伙伴，它一直陪伴索夫罗娜的身边，直到索夫罗娜黄黑色的头发开始混着灰色。
